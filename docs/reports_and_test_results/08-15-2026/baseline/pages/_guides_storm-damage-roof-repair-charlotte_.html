Hail and wind damage in Charlotte

Charlotte sits in an active severe-weather corridor. Every spring and summer, hail and straight-line winds move through the metro, and roofs take the hit. The frustrating part is that storm damage rarely announces itself — you might not see a single missing shingle from the driveway while your roof quietly starts to fail. That’s why the smart move after a storm is a close, on-the-roof look from someone who knows what to look for. Safe Haven has spent six years inspecting Charlotte roofs after storms, we’re fully insured, and the inspection is free.

What storm damage actually looks like

Up close, hail leaves bruises and knocks the protective granules off the shingle surface, exposing the asphalt underneath and shortening the roof’s life. Wind lifts and creases shingles or tears them off outright, and it can loosen the flashing around chimneys and vents. You might also see dented gutters, dinged vent caps, or the first signs of a leak — a stain on a ceiling or in the attic. Some of this is visible from the ground; a lot of it isn’t. We document all of it with photos so you have a clear record.

What to do right after a storm

First, stay safe — don’t climb up there yourself. If water is actively getting in, we can get out quickly and tarp the roof to stop the damage from spreading while you sort out next steps. Then get the roof inspected and documented while the damage is fresh. The sooner it’s on record, the simpler everything downstream is. Be careful who you hand the job to, too — storms bring out-of-town crews with them. Our guide to choosing a roofing contractor covers what to ask.

How we help with your insurance

If you decide to go through insurance, here’s what we do: we inspect the roof, document the damage with photos, and provide our own estimate and scope of the work. If it helps, we can meet your adjuster on-site so everyone is looking at the same roof and the same information, and we’ll answer your questions along the way. Your deductible is your responsibility — that’s set by your policy. You can read more on our insurance claims page.

Repair or replace after a storm?

Not every storm means a new roof. Sometimes a handful of shingles and some flashing is all it takes; sometimes the damage is widespread enough that replacement is the better value.
