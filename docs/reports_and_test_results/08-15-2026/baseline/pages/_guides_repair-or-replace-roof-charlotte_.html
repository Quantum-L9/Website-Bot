The repair-or-replace question

It’s one of the most common questions we get in Charlotte: is this a repair, or is it time for a whole new roof? The honest answer depends on a few things — how old the roof is, how much of it is damaged, and whether you’re fixing the same spot over and over. Here’s how to think about it, and how we help you land on the right call for your home and budget.

When a repair makes sense

If your roof is relatively young and healthy and the problem is limited — a few shingles blown off in a storm, a leak around a single vent or valley, some flashing that’s come loose — a targeted repair is almost always the smarter spend. There’s no reason to replace a good roof over a small, fixable problem. We fix it, back the work with our five-year workmanship warranty, and you get years more out of the roof you already have.

When it’s time to replace

Replacement starts making sense when the damage is widespread or the roof is simply worn out. Shingles that are curling, cracking, or losing granules across the whole roof, bald patches, daylight in the attic, or leaks in more than one place are all signs the roof is near the end. If you find yourself calling for another repair every season, those repairs add up — and at some point a new roof is the better value than chasing the next leak.

Roof age and what it means

Most asphalt-shingle roofs in the Charlotte area last somewhere in the fifteen-to-twenty-five-year range, depending on the product and how they’ve weathered our summers. If your roof is pushing that age and starting to show problems, replacement is often the better long-term value even if a repair would technically hold for now. A metal roof lasts longer — we install GAF, CertainTeed, and Atlas shingles as well as metal, and our metal versus shingles guide walks through how the two compare for a Charlotte home.

How we help you decide

You don’t have to guess. We’ll get on your roof, take a close look, show you photos of what we find, and give you a straight recommendation — repair or replace, whichever actually serves you. It isn’t in our interest to sell you a roof you don’t need. If cost is the question, our roof replacement cost guide breaks down what drives the price, and if a storm’s involved, see our storm damage guide.

Do I Need a New Roof or a Repair? — FAQ

How long does a roof last in Charlotte?+

Most asphalt-shingle roofs here last somewhere in the fifteen-to-twenty-five-year range, depending on the product and how they've weathered our summers. Metal roofs last longer.

When is a roof repair enough?+

When the roof is otherwise healthy and the problem is limited — a few blown-off shingles, a single leak, some loose flashing. There's no reason to replace a good roof over a small, fixable problem.

When should I replace instead of repair?+

When the damage is widespread or the roof is worn out — shingles curling or losing granules across the whole roof, leaks in more than one place, or repairs every season that keep adding up.

Can you tell me which one I need?+

Yes — that's what the free inspection is for. We'll get on your roof, show you photos of what we find, and give you a straight recommendation, not the most expensive one.
